## Your Use Case ELIMINATES Foam Core

### Rocky Shore + "Beat It Up" = Foam Core Disaster

**What happens when you drag foam-core hull over rocks:**

1. **First launch:** Sharp rock punctures fiberglass skin
2. **Water intrudes:** Foam starts absorbing water (adds 5-10 lbs)
3. **Impact damage:** Foam crushes under point loads
4. **Fiberglass delaminates:** Constant flexing separates glass from foam
5. **Six months later:** Squishy spots, waterlogged sections, performance degraded
6. **Repair attempt:** Grind out damage, dry foam (takes weeks), re-glass ($$$)
7. **One year later:** Multiple soft spots, considering rebuild

**Foam core works for:** Smooth ramps, gentle use, trailer launching  
**Foam core DOES NOT work for:** Rocky shores, "beat it up" use, expedition conditions

**Verdict: Foam core is WRONG for your use case.**

---

## The Solution: Riveted Aluminum (No Welding Required)

### You Don't NEED to Weld Aluminum

**Alternative: Solid aluminum rivets**

**How it works:**
1. Drill holes through overlapping panels
2. Insert solid aluminum rivet
3. Set with rivet gun (squeeze handle, rivet expands)
4. Creates permanent, watertight joint

**Used in:**
- Every aluminum canoe (Grumman, etc.)
- Aircraft fuselages (Boeing, Airbus)
- Aluminum jon boats
- Military landing craft

**This is PROVEN technology for 100+ years.**

# Part 2: Rivet Waterproofing - Complete Technical Analysis

## Can 400 Holes Be Watertight?

**Your concern is 100% valid:** "How can drilling 400 holes through aluminum hull possibly be watertight?"

**Short answer:** It can be, but requires proper technique.

---

## The Three-Layer Waterproofing System

### Wet Riveting (STANDARD MARINE PRACTICE)

**Layer 1: Sealant Between Panels**
- Apply 3M 5200 marine sealant BEFORE assembling
- Run bead along entire seam (1/4" to 1/2" wide)
- Panels compress sealant when clamped
- Excess squeeze-out = good seal

**Layer 2: Sealant Around Rivet Shaft**
- Dab sealant into each drilled hole
- On BOTH sides of joint
- Rivet squeezes through sealant on insertion
- Fills microscopic gap between rivet and hole

**Layer 3: Sealant Over Rivet Heads**
- Apply small dab over each rivet head after setting
- Creates final gasket around rivet
- Belt-and-suspenders approach

**Result:**
- Sealant between panels
- Sealant around each rivet shaft  
- Sealant over rivet heads
- Three layers of waterproofing

**This is how aircraft fuel tanks are sealed.**

**Success rate:** 99%+ watertight if done correctly

---

## Rivet Types (CRITICAL)

### ✅ Use: Solid Aluminum Rivets

**Construction:**
- Solid shaft (no hollow core)
- Installed with rivet gun + bucking bar
- Expands to fill hole when set

**Waterproofing:**
- ✅ Rivet itself is watertight (solid metal)
- With sealant: Perfect seal

**Cost:** $0.10 each × 400 = $40

---

### ❌ DON'T Use: Pop Rivets

**Construction:**
- Hollow shaft with mandrel hole

**Problems:**
- ❌ Hollow core = direct water path
- ❌ Mandrel hole in center
- ❌ Water can wick through even with sealant

**Verdict:** DO NOT USE for below-waterline applications

---

## Sealant Selection

### 3M 5200 (RECOMMENDED)

**Properties:**
- Polyurethane-based
- Permanent bond (very difficult to remove)
- Flexible when cured (absorbs vibration)
- UV resistant
- Temperature range: -60°F to 250°F

**Cure time:**
- Skin over: 24 hours
- Full cure: 5-7 days

**Cost:** $15 per 3 oz tube (need 5-6 tubes = $80)

**Verdict:** Use this for hull seams

---

## Rivet Spacing and Pattern

### Optimal Spacing: 1.5" to 2" apart

**Why:**
- ✅ Distributes load evenly
- ✅ Prevents panel separation
- ✅ Sealant can bridge gap
- ✅ Standard marine practice

### Double Row Staggered Pattern (BEST)

```
Panel 1  ████████████████████
         ●   ●   ●   ●   ●    ← Row 1
Overlap  
           ●   ●   ●   ●      ← Row 2 (offset)
Panel 2  ████████████████████
```

**For 16' proa:**
- Keel seam: Double row, staggered
- Chine seams: Double row, staggered
- Bulkheads: Single row (adequate)

---

## Testing Waterproofness

### Pre-Launch: Flood Test

**Process:**
1. Plug all openings
2. Fill hull with water (6" depth)
3. Let sit 24 hours
4. Inspect underneath for drips
5. Mark any wet spots
6. Dry hull, apply sealant to leaking rivets
7. Re-test

**Expected result:**
- Properly wet-riveted hull: ZERO leaks
- Poor technique: 5-10% of rivets may seep
- After touch-up: 100% watertight
